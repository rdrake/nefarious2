A reconnecting phone gets its whole catch-up at once

The ircd slows every client to about one command every 2 seconds once its first few are used. This change lets a logged-in client borrow from a shared pool that the server refills every minute. On a quiet server, a reconnect's burst is answered straight away. When many clients connect at once, the pool runs dry and they get what they get today.

The pool during the run

This is a model of the ircd's read loop, ticking once a second. The branch measured, with 40 PINGs: 14 replies at once and the last after 52 s today, all 40 at once with the pool, and 9 to 10 of 20 clients in full during a storm.

One command at a time

Each command's lag is paid from three places, in order. Only the middle one is new. Send commands and watch which one pays.

Own gracePOSTREG_GRACE
Borrowedfrom the pool, 30 s window
Clock ahead of nowreading stops at 10 s

1

The client's own grace. The 20 s credit every logged-in connection already gets at registration. Unchanged.

2

The shared pool, new. For 30 seconds after registration, a logged-in client may borrow up to its allowance from a server-wide pool that refills in a minute. It takes only what it actually spends, so a client with one channel barely touches it.

3

The client's clock. What's left goes on the clock as it does today. The server stops reading a client once its clock is 10 s ahead, then lets one more line through every 2 s.

What doesn't change

  • Off by defaultBoth settings default to 0, and then the server behaves exactly as it does today.
  • Anonymous clientsThey never borrow and keep the classic burst of about 5 commands, so the flood surface stays the same.
  • The 10-second gateThe rule that stops reading a client's lines is untouched. Borrowing only keeps the clock lower.
  • Every other limitTarget limiting, receive queue caps, channel modes and the multiline, METADATA and BATCH limits all still apply.

How it was checked

  • ProvedWith CBMC: for any input, the pool stays between 0 and its cap and nothing overflows. A clock jump backward adds nothing. A command never costs more than it does today, and with the allowance at 0 the cost is exactly today's.
  • ProvedWith z3: over a run of any length, the pool lends at most its cap per minute.
  • SanitizedASan, UBSan and valgrind on the unit tests. ASan and UBSan on a live server during a 20-client storm. No reports.
  • Analyzedgcc's analyzer and cppcheck are clean. scan-build finds the same 115 warnings as the base commit, none new.
  • Tested25 unit tests, and two independent code reviews.

One catch for Seance

Seance paces itself by its own model of the server's clock and never sends past it. It doesn't know about the grace or the pool, so it won't use the extra headroom until a small per-network setting tells it. Clients that send everything at once, which is most desktop clients, benefit straight away.
